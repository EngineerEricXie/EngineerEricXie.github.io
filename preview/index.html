02 / Engineering

Featured build

The simulator behind the data: a coupled flow-and-transport framework I built for branching biological geometries, from CT or microscopy reconstruction to GPU solve.

Open source · CoupledFlow

GPU-accelerated isogeometric flow and transport for branching neurons

A C++ framework for coupled flow and transport across 0D, 1D and 3D domains: lumped circuits, vascular network models, tetrahedral finite elements and body-fitted isogeometric analysis, with multispecies transport, moving domains and checkpoint/restart. CPU solvers run on MPI and PETSc; the IGA backend also runs on a single CUDA GPU.

Against the HexSim CPU workflow on three branching-neuron geometries, the optimized CPU path is 1.4–2.7× faster and the V100 backend 1.8–9.1× faster end to end. On the GPU the gain comes from assembly: 5.5–24.5× faster than 8–16 MPI ranks, at roughly half the peak memory.

03 / Publications

Selected papers

First-author work in bold. The full, automatically updated list is on Google Scholar.

  1. arXiv2026 · preprint

    MGRD: Compact morphology-gated residual diffusion for variance-aware cross-domain neurite forecasting

    T.Y. Hsieh, C. Anitescu, C. Kim, V.A. Webster-Wood, Y.J. Zhang
    arXiv preprint, 2026

    Forecasts 20 future neurite-morphology frames from 10 observations, with repeated sampling to estimate forecast variability. Reduces trajectory-wise mean MAE by 39.6% and improves skeleton F1 by 45.3% over gSTA on human iPSC-derived neuron microscopy, using 1.01% of its parameters.

    arXiv
  2. bioRxiv2026 · preprint

    Predicting macroscopic axon topology from microscopic kinematics: an interactive tracking and random walk pipeline for substrate-dependent cortical neurospheres

    C. Kim, M. Kim, H. Cao, T.Y. Hsieh, Y.J. Zhang, T. Cohen-Karni, V. Webster-Wood
    bioRxiv, 2026

    Links single-axon growth dynamics to network-scale topology; the generative model reproduces both microscopic behavior and macroscopic axonal density.

    bioRxiv
